# FlowerMarketOntology Deep Research: First Collection Batch and Research Program

## Research frame, evidence rules, and tested assumptions

The attached `research-roadmap.md` is treated as the governing brief. Its required sequence is: Persian discovery; names and identity; care literature; search/navigation; decision support; floristry/commerce. It also requires evidence states of `reported`, `verified`, and `unavailable`; preservation of original Persian labels and normalized forms; Tehran-first sampling; and explicit retention of ambiguity rather than forced taxonomic resolution. fileciteturn0file0

This research therefore uses four provenance classes inside those roadmap statuses. **Direct observation** means material I could retrieve from the seller or authoritative source during this research on **2026-11-12**. **Supplied-report lead** means something stated by the roadmap or an antecedent report but not independently visible here. **External research** means authoritative botanical, horticultural, toxicological, or market-source material retrieved independently. **Inference** is a conclusion drawn from those observations and is explicitly marked as such. No inaccessible page, seller stock level, demand level, market share, wholesale price, or product availability is represented as observed.

The roadmap's working assumption—“Persian-first apartment growers in Iran, English edition later, Tehran first”—survives this first batch only as a **reasonable operational hypothesis**, not as a proven market fact. The named Iranian sources are overwhelmingly Persian-language and prominently use apartment-plant concepts such as «گیاهان آپارتمانی», while Tarhegol and VIPShop expose Tehran-specific delivery propositions. A Mashhad seller channel also uses Persian apartment-plant terminology, suggesting the vocabulary is not uniquely Tehran-specific. That supports Persian-first information architecture and Tehran-first *sampling*, but it does **not** establish audience size, demand share, conversion behavior, or the relative importance of Tehran versus other cities. citeturn310404search8turn119771search7turn820211search2

A second assumption also needs correction: retailer categories should be treated as **lexical and navigation evidence**, not botanical truth. The first batch already produces two strong counterexamples. Iranian usage of «برگ انجیری» spans *Monstera deliciosa* and a deeply lobed philodendron-type plant, while «بابا آدم» is used as a market name for *Alocasia* without necessarily specifying species. The ontology therefore cannot safely collapse a Persian common name directly onto one taxon ID. citeturn200935search0turn200935search4turn200935search5

## Pilot profile and ambiguous-name workflow test

### Pilot profile: پتوس

**Stable profile concept:** `plant-fa-pothos-001`  
**Display label:** پتوس  
**Normalized Persian aliases:** پتوس; پوتوس  
**Current identity decision:** principal taxon candidate *Epipremnum aureum* (Linden & André) G.S.Bunting; accepted by Kew. Kew also records *Pothos aureus*, *Rhaphidophora aurea* and *Scindapsus aureus* as synonyms, which explains some historical naming drift. citeturn981170search0

**Identity claim — verified.** The standard horticultural pothos represented by “golden pothos” is *Epipremnum aureum*. NC State explicitly uses common names including Pothos, Golden Pothos and Devil's Ivy and notes that *E. pinnatum* ‘Aureum’ is an older treatment rather than the current preferred species concept. citeturn724840search2

**Light claim — evidence-backed, apartment-applicable.** NC State says the species prefers bright indirect light but can survive long periods in low light; RHS similarly says bright indirect light is preferred, some shade is tolerated, deep shade can weaken growth/variegation, and strong direct summer sun can scorch foliage. The user-facing profile should therefore say something like: **“Best in bright indirect light; tolerates lower light, but growth and variegation may decline. Protect from strong direct sun.”** This is stronger than Tarhegol's seller wording because it reconciles two horticultural sources. citeturn724840search2turn469743search2

**Watering claim — evidence-backed, condition-based rather than calendar-based.** NC State recommends allowing the well-drained medium to dry between watering; RHS says water when compost is approaching dryness and warns that persistently wet compost can cause root rot. Therefore the ontology should store the decision rule **“water after partial drying”**, with calendar frequencies relegated to seller observations or contextual examples, not universal care truth. citeturn724840search2turn469743search2

**Humidity claim — conflicting emphasis, reconciled.** NC State suggests increasing humidity with a humidifier or pebble tray, whereas RHS explicitly notes that epipremnums do not require the high humidity demanded by many tropical houseplants. A defensible summary is: **“Normal household humidity is generally adequate; additional humidity may be helpful in very dry interiors but is not a core requirement.”** That synthesis should be marked `reviewed_synthesis`, not copied as a direct quotation. citeturn724840search2turn469743search2

**Substrate/repotting claim — evidence-backed.** RHS recommends an open, moisture-retentive but well-drained slightly acidic medium and warns against overpotting because excessive wet retention raises root-rot risk; it suggests repotting when roots become crowded. The profile should store drainage and root-crowding conditions, not a rigid annual schedule. citeturn469743search2

**Toxicity claim — species-specific.** ASPCA identifies *Epipremnum aureum* as toxic to dogs and cats because of insoluble calcium oxalates; signs include oral irritation, drooling, vomiting and difficulty swallowing. NC State independently labels the plant poisonous. For ontology purposes, the toxic principle and affected populations should be stored separately from the broad boolean `toxic=true`. citeturn466382search0turn724840search2

**Retail contrast — seller claim only.** Tarhegol's current «پتوس ابلق» product says «نور غیر مستقیم متوسط تا زیاد، آبیاری دو بار در هفته». That is useful evidence of Persian market phrasing, but the fixed twice-weekly frequency should not become the canonical care rule because RHS and NC State support moisture-state-based watering instead. citeturn310404search0turn469743search2turn724840search2

A claim-level record for that watering conclusion should therefore contain fields equivalent to: `profile_id`, `topic=watering`, `condition="container-grown indoor E. aureum; well-drained medium"`, `recommendation="allow medium to partially dry before rewatering"`, `sources=[NC State, RHS]`, `conflict="retailer gives fixed twice-weekly schedule"`, `applicability="apartment interiors"`, `review_status="reviewed"`, and an evidence timestamp of **2026-11-12**. That model directly implements the roadmap's reproducibility requirement. fileciteturn0file0

### Ambiguity test: برگ‌انجیری

The workflow should begin with the raw query `برگ انجیری`, preserve the orthographic form, normalize it to `برگ‌انجیری` for canonical display/search, and retrieve seller/context evidence before assigning a species. Current evidence yields at least two candidate branches. A plain Nargil retail result strongly favors *Monstera deliciosa*, while «فیلودندرون برگ انجیری» and Tarhegol's «برگ انجیری پنجه شیری» demonstrate a separate philodendron-like concept. citeturn200935search4turn200935search3turn200935search0

Kew then validates candidate taxon identities independently: *Monstera deliciosa* is accepted, and *Philodendron bipinnatifidum* is currently accepted in Kew's backbone, with *Thaumatophyllum bipinnatifidum* treated there as a synonym. citeturn981170search3turn188937search3turn188937search0

The correct workflow result is therefore **unresolved at the base-label level**. A product explicitly saying «برگ انجیری مانسترا» may be resolved to *M. deliciosa* with substantially higher confidence; a record saying only «برگ انجیری» should retain multiple candidate IDs. This is not a failure of the ontology. It is the ontology faithfully representing market language.

The same workflow must be used for «بابا آدم». Nargil provides strong evidence that Iranian sellers often mean an *Alocasia*, but Kew recognizes distinct *Alocasia macrorrhizos*, *A. odora* and *Colocasia esculenta*, all capable of participating in “elephant ear” naming traditions. A species assignment based solely on «بابا آدم» would therefore be unsupported. citeturn200935search5turn724840search0turn188937search1turn724840search1

## Remaining roadmap phases: feasibility, evidence design, and risks

### Search and navigation

This phase should begin earlier than the roadmap's numbering suggests because the first batch already demonstrates high-value variant pairs: `پتوس / پوتوس`, `زاموفیلیا / زامیفولیا`, `برگ‌انجیری / برگ انجیری`, `سانسوریا / سانسوريا`, and scientific/legacy names such as *Dracaena trifasciata / Sansevieria trifasciata*. Kew's taxonomic synonymy and Iranian spelling variation make synonym-aware retrieval essential rather than optional. citeturn981170search1turn310404search5turn922277search2

The required roadmap queries—«سانسوريا», «برگ انجیری», «نور کم», «زرد شدن برگ»—should be tested with a fixture model that records raw query, normalized query, expansion terms, expected concept IDs, returned concept IDs, ranking reason and whether ambiguity was surfaced. fileciteturn0file0

A key risk is **over-normalization**. Joining spelling variants is desirable; joining semantically different market names is not. `پتوس` and `پوتوس` can normally share a normalized search cluster, while `برگ انجیری` should not be normalized into `Monstera deliciosa` because direct seller evidence contradicts that one-to-one assumption. citeturn200935search0turn200935search4

### Decision support

Yellow leaves, wilt and leaf spots should be modeled as **symptoms**, not diagnoses. Even the current pothos sources show that overwatering can produce root rot, leaf-margin blackening or yellowing, while poor light can affect variegation; a symptom engine therefore needs environmental conditions and competing causes rather than “symptom → one answer.” citeturn724840search2

Fixtures should contain known/unknown dimensions for light exposure, substrate moisture, drainage, temperature, recent repotting, fertilization, pest signs and affected tissue. The exit criterion should not be “the engine produces an answer”; it should be “the engine keeps more than one cause when evidence is insufficient and asks for the variable with highest expected disambiguation value.” This is an inference from the roadmap's explicit requirement to handle ambiguous cases and missing data. fileciteturn0file0

### Floristry and commerce

VIPShop is a productive first florist-language source because it exposes arrangement form, occasion, flower-type filters, same-day/quick-order semantics and delivery-zone terminology on one seller. Arrangement form should therefore be a distinct ontology facet from botanical ingredient: `دسته گل`, `باکس گل`, `سبد گل`, `تاج گل`, and `گل ترحیم` are commerce/design concepts, whereas `رز`, `گلایل`, `لیسیانتوس`, `لیلیوم`, etc. are ingredient/taxon-linked concepts. citeturn188356search0turn188356search1turn188356search2

Substitution needs explicit evidence. VIPShop says flower composition can depend on season, inventory and design, which means a bouquet should not be represented as a guaranteed static ingredient list unless the individual product contract says so. citeturn188356search1

### Mahallati and Imam Reza/Khavaran

Current public web material supports treating both as important wholesale research targets, but it is not sufficient for the roadmap's required unit/seasonality evidence. Recent secondary sources characterize Mahallati as heavily wholesale while allowing retail purchases and Imam Reza/Khavaran as a major wholesale market for cut flowers and other plant products. A direct seller site, Nora Gol, explicitly describes its cut-flower catalogue as wholesale distribution direct from greenhouse to Imam Reza market. citeturn931193search2turn931193search3turn931193search5

There is also a current Imam Reza market Telegram feed showing price-list language and units such as «مریم(25شاخه)» and «آفتاب گردان(25 شاخه)». That is valuable as a **lead**, but such ephemeral posts require timestamped capture and seller/stall attribution before being promoted to verified wholesale-unit records. citeturn931193search6

The wholesale fieldwork should therefore record `market`, `hall/aisle/stall`, `seller`, `capture_datetime`, `flower`, `cultivar/color`, `grade`, `unit_text_raw`, `unit_count`, `bundle/container`, `price`, `currency`, `wholesale_minimum`, `retail_allowed`, `origin`, `seasonality_claim`, and `source_permission`. Without these fields, a number such as “350,000” is semantically brittle because one cannot know whether it refers to a stem, bunch, 25-stem bundle, tray or other unit. This is an inference supported by the observed bundled-unit wording. citeturn931193search6

### English-market expansion

The English comparison should not simply translate Persian labels. The Sill emphasizes product attributes, size/planter variants and “How It Arrives”; Patch emphasizes care continuity and named plant merchandising; Bloom & Wild frames plants more strongly as gifts with delivery-date and card options. Those different content models justify locale-specific concept labels and merchandising facets even where the underlying botanical taxon is identical. citeturn502896search1turn502896search6turn502896search5

English expansion should therefore occur after the identity layer is stable enough to separate **locale-specific common names** from globally shared taxon IDs. The taxon can be shared; labels, search aliases, arrangement concepts, care phrasing, fulfillment and commerce taxonomies should remain locale/market scoped.

## Evidence schema, gaps, priorities, and explicit next actions

The existing roadmap evidence categories are sound, but the data model needs stronger provenance and ambiguity support. The core recommendation is to separate **observations**, **claims**, **entities**, and **decisions** rather than letting one record serve all four roles. This follows directly from the current seller conflicts and taxonomic ambiguity. fileciteturn0file0

A practical schema is:

| Record type | Minimum fields |
|---|---|
| `source_capture` | `capture_id`, `source_url`, `source_owner`, `market`, `city`, `accessed_at`, `capture_method`, `http/access_status`, `evidence_status`, `raw_excerpt`, `snapshot_ref`, `rights_notes` |
| `seller_observation` | `observation_id`, `capture_id`, `raw_label`, `parent_path[]`, `ui_role`, `product_or_listing`, `price_raw`, `currency`, `delivery_raw`, `mapped_concept_id`, `mapping_confidence` |
| `name_alias` | `alias_id`, `raw_name`, `normalized_name`, `locale`, `script`, `name_type`, `market_scope`, `candidate_taxa[]`, `precision`, `evidence_refs[]`, `decision_status` |
| `taxon_candidate` | `candidate_id`, `accepted_name`, `authority`, `rank`, `family`, `kew_id`, `synonyms[]`, `taxonomic_status`, `checked_at` |
| `identity_decision` | `alias_id`, `candidate_id`, `decision={accepted,rejected,possible}`, `confidence`, `basis`, `reviewer`, `reviewed_at` |
| `care_claim` | `claim_id`, `profile_id`, `topic`, `conditions`, `population_or_context`, `recommendation`, `source_id`, `source_location`, `evidence_strength`, `conflicts[]`, `review_status`, `reviewer`, `reviewed_at` |

Three fields should be mandatory nearly everywhere: **`observed_at/accessed_at`**, **`provenance_type`**, and **`evidence_status`**. Without time, prices and delivery terms silently decay; without provenance, a seller claim can masquerade as horticultural guidance; without status, “not found” can be mistaken for “does not exist.”

The largest present gaps are material. The historical **existing Sill capture itself was not supplied**, so delta analysis is impossible. Nargil's current delivery terms were not recovered. Bloom & Wild did not yield a sufficiently stable species-level sample in the accessible capture. The first regional seller comparison is only one Mashhad seller channel and cannot establish regional norms. Mahallati and Imam Reza/Khavaran have not yet been sampled stall-by-stall with verified unit semantics. No actual user search test has yet been run, and no evidence-backed symptom graph has been built. These should remain explicit `unavailable` or `not_yet_collected`, not silently filled from secondary summaries. fileciteturn0file0
